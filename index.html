2025– Flagship, launched April 2026

Packleads

A platform that measures how visible a local business really is. It checks the things a buyer would otherwise check by hand, scores them, and turns whatever is missing into a report you can put in front of a client.

packleads.io

4,478 businesses measured, across 18,792 US cities

How it was built

Problem

Before engineering I was a web designer, and finding clients was the hardest part of the job. I spent nights scrolling Google Maps guessing which local businesses had weak websites and which were worth pitching. I was the customer before I was the founder.

Build

Next.js 14 and TypeScript on Supabase with row level security, Redis on Upstash for caching and rate limiting, Stripe for credit based billing. Natural language search runs a plain English brief through an OpenAI query planner, which compiles it into a structured query against live business data. A registry of 19 analyzers runs over a single crawl and produces a 0 to 100 visibility score. Adding a signal is one registry entry, not another pass over the data.

Result

1,200 leads processed in the first twelve weeks. 4,478 businesses measured across 18,792 US cities, with 7,197 review snapshots collected over time, which is data you can only build by watching rather than looking up.

Built withNext.js, TypeScript, React, Tailwind, Supabase, PostgreSQL, Redis, Stripe, OpenAI, Leaflet, Playwright, Vercel

  3. 2022–24 Serial number recognition Cameras that read stamped, etched, and handwritten serial numbers off equipment while the line keeps moving. 98% accuracy Oshkosh

    Problem

    Serial capture was manual. Someone read a number off a part and typed it in, which is slow at best and quietly wrong at worst.

  4. 2025 PulseHue Room lighting that follows what is playing, without a round trip through a vendor cloud. 700ms end to end Personal

    Problem

    Every ambient lighting integration I could find sent commands through a vendor cloud, which meant the lights were always a beat behind the music.

    Build

    A Node.js and Express service reading live Spotify playback over OAuth 2.0 and driving Govee lights directly over the LAN. Dominant color extraction from album art with HSL to RGB mapping picks the scene. Lights that are powered off or drop off the network are retried rather than allowed to wedge the loop.

    Result

    About 700ms from a track change to a light change, and the remaining latency is Spotify polling rather than anything on the device path.

    Built withNode.js, Express, Spotify Web API, OAuth 2.0, Govee LAN API, REST

One incident, if you only read one thing

What broke was station control no longer registering when a vehicle arrived, so it surfaced as station logic misbehaving rather than as missing data. Approach

Software that matters is software somebody is depending on right now.

Where it starts

I grew up in the Democratic Republic of the Congo until I was seven, without much access to technology. Getting to it late is why building for the web has never stopped feeling like something worth being careful about. I do not take a working system for granted, because I remember not having one.

Who it is for

The people using what I build at work are plant operators and maintenance technicians, reading a screen mid shift with a line waiting on them. Watching someone depend on your interface under pressure permanently changes how you think about a loading state, an error message, and where the important number goes.

How I find problems

By being in them. Packleads exists because I was the web designer scrolling maps for leads. Sellorie exists because I asked the floor staff what was breaking before I asked the manager. You only learn the real constraint by talking to the people doing the work.

What I hold to

If I built it, I stand behind it. That means writing the runbook as well as the service, adding the alert after the incident, and telling you plainly what I have not done yet. Overselling gets found out in week two, and I would rather be the person you keep calling.

Contact.

I work on backend, full stack, and platform, and anywhere real time data has to reach a person who is about to make a decision with it. I am in Maryland, four years of that remote, and I am glad to be in the DC and Baltimore corridor.
